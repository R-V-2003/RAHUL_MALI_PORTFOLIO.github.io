Overview

What is PTENote?

PTENote is an online platform that helps students prepare for the Pearson Test of English (PTE) Academic exam. It provides practice tests, score predictions, and study resources for test-takers globally.

I was tasked with completely redesigning their marketing website during my internship at Sedani Digital — improving the visual design, information architecture, responsive layout, and overall user experience to drive trust and conversions.

Problem

What problems existed?

Prehistoric Visuals

The original UI looked like it survived the ice age. It didn't scream "professional exam prep"—it screamed "designed in MS Paint by my neighbor's tech-savvy cousin back in 2004".

Brain-Melting Content

The content was so dry and dense it could cure insomnia. It dumped text walls directly on users, completely ignoring the fact that our attention span is now shorter than a goldfish on TikTok.

Hide-and-Seek Mobile Nav

Finding other pages was a hide-and-seek game. The mobile menu was so poorly optimized that critical sections (vouchers, mock tests) were practically in the witness protection program.

Sketchy Transaction Vibes

It's a paid platform asking for actual money, yet the old layout felt so untrustworthy that visitors probably checked their credit cards for unauthorized charges immediately.

Process

How I approached the redesign

1. Research & Audit

Audited the existing PTENote website to identify pain points. Analyzed competitor platforms (Magoosh, E2Language, MockPTE) for UX patterns. Documented gaps in information hierarchy, mobile usability, and conversion flows.

2. Wireframing & Information Architecture

Created low-fidelity wireframes in Figma mapping out the new page structure: hero → social proof → features → pricing → CTA. Focused on reducing cognitive load and guiding users toward signup.

3. High-Fidelity Design in Figma

Designed the complete UI in Figma with a refreshed color palette, modern typography (Inter + Poppins), glassmorphism cards, and a clear visual hierarchy. Created component variants for responsive breakpoints (desktop, tablet, mobile).

4. Iterative Development & AI Loops

Since we leverage AI for design and development, we followed a strictly iterative model. A great product is not built in one shot—it requires continuous cycles of design refinement, testing, and development to improve user flows compared to the older versions.

5. Testing & Launch

Tested across Chrome, Safari, Firefox on desktop and mobile. Validated accessibility with keyboard navigation and screen reader checks. Optimized images and loading performance before deployment.

Solution

The final design

The redesigned PTENote platform transitions to a high-converting, highly engaging experience. By combining user psychology with modern UI patterns, the new version delivers:

Sarcastic Copywriting

We dumped the dry academic jargon and rewrote the text with actual humor and sarcasm. It keeps student attention longer than 3 seconds, which is a massive upgrade over standard boring landing pages.

Animated Illustrations

Instead of hitting users with dense text walls, we used clean animated illustrations to explain complex stuff. Because let's face it: why read when you can look at pretty moving pictures?

Human Reels for Trust

We embedded video reels featuring real humans explaining PTENote. It turns out students trust a real person's face way more than they trust a generic corporate logo. Shocking, right?

Bottom Navigation Bar

We put navigation tabs at the bottom on mobile, just like a social media app. Now users click other pages out of sheer habit and curiosity, instead of getting lost in a hidden mobile menu.

Mobile Interface Redesign: Features engaging mascot illustrations, clean registration forms, custom colors, and the accessible bottom navigation bar.
App-Like Bottom Navigation: Puts core pages directly in the "Thumb Zone," avoiding thumb gymnastics required by traditional top-corner menus.
Social App Familiarity: Mimics mainstream apps (Instagram, YouTube) where users spend 90% of screen time, driving clicks out of habit.
No More Digital Dust: Keeps secondary booking services (Vouchers, Mock Tests) visible continuously to increase clicks and conversions.

Reflection

What I learned

Designing in Figma ≠ building in browser. Many decisions I made in Figma needed refinement during development — spacing felt different, animations needed tuning, and real content (not lorem ipsum) changed the visual weight of sections.

Iterative design is the way. The final result came from constant back-and-forth between Figma and code — not from a single "hand-off" moment. This taught me the value of being both the designer and developer.

Performance matters. Optimizing images, reducing layout shifts, and ensuring fast load times are just as important as the visual design itself.
